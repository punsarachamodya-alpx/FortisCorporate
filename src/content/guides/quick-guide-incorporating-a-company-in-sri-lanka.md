Setting up a company in Sri Lanka involves a handful of core steps: choosing and reserving a company name with the Registrar of Companies, preparing the constitutional documents (Articles of Association), appointing directors and a company secretary, deciding on the initial shareholding structure, and submitting the incorporation application with the required supporting documents.

Foreign investors and overseas businesses have some additional considerations, including sector-specific approvals, BOI-related registrations where relevant, and decisions around the most appropriate structure — a locally incorporated company, a branch office, or a liaison office.

Once incorporated, a company also takes on ongoing statutory obligations — annual return filings, maintaining statutory registers, and keeping corporate records up to date — which is where ongoing company secretarial support becomes useful.
